Roof and passenger flows. Singapore's new terminal for 50 million passengers a year, designed with Heatherwick Studio.

The design treats the terminal as a piece of the city rather than a machine for moving people. I worked on passenger flows and on the roof, shaping it with hand-placed control points and Grasshopper scripts for structure, glazing and drainage, inside a grid that fixed where its peaks and valleys could fall.

In 2018 I also tested early machine learning on the roof. It fell short, and the design still came down to judgment.

Facade lead. A 36-story office tower where Old Street meets the City of London.

Writer, director, animator. My MArch thesis film at the Bartlett: ten minutes inside a world that knows it was built.

Architectural sets, turned inside out and partly inhabited, recombine across an expanded East London site until they start to generate a fiction of their own. Drawing on Charlie Kaufman and Jorge Luis Borges, the film leans into its own artificiality, then slows down for small moments of design: objects that put you in touch with real materials, and with other worlds.

I built the world, wrote the script and voiceover, planned the camera, made the sound and edited the film, alongside a 10,000-word thesis on reflexive worldbuilding.

With heights capped, the challenge became breaking down three very long buildings. Urban design and visuals. A competition-winning plan to turn two south London shopping centers into a mixed-use town center.

The proposal turns the Whitgift Centre and Centrale into denser, mixed-use blocks, with streets and spaces drawn from the area's character. Reusing existing site elements cut carbon and kept some of the neighborhood's identity.

I led parts of the urban design, designed facades and public spaces, tested the massing through view analysis and directed the visualization.

Bartlett design project. A museum in Deptford, London, that absorbs other museums, after the recursive ornament of Bavarian Rococo churches.
